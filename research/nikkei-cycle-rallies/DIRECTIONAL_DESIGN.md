# Directional evidence persistence, final bounded follow-up

The strongest confirmation family in the prior disclosed round is directional
evidence. Its instant release when ADX falls below 20 is conceptually weak:
ADX measures strength, not direction, and can decline during a continuing rise.
This final follow-up keeps the same screened candidates, dates, 20/40, 40/80
and 60/120 pairs and tests two persistence rules, declared before execution:

- Both selected longer strategies long, +DI14>-DI14 and ADX14>=20 confirms
  full target. Two consecutive closes with -DI14>=+DI14 OR both longer
  strategies cash releases to the ten-session target. Otherwise hold at least
  the ten-session target. A decline in ADX alone does not force an exit.
- The same rule without an ADX strength threshold tests the faster, less
  selective directional confirmation; direction and both longer votes remain
  required. This is an explicit sensitivity/control, not a fitted threshold.

There is still one target/ledger. All six results are retained beside the
original nine and fifteen quality variants. No further threshold search is
performed in this round. Results remain retrospective. Primary-source ADX
semantics and the complete experiment ledger accompany the final report.
